# PROVENANCE & LICENSING

This document records exactly what ships in this repository, where it comes
from, and under what terms — so the project can be reasoned about legally and
historically.

## 1. The base operating system

- **What:** FreeBSD 2.2.8-RELEASE for i386 (with FreeBSD 4.11-RELEASE held as a
  documented fallback target).
- **License:** the BSD license. FreeBSD is freely redistributable. **However,**
  this repository does **not** redistribute it. The build pipeline *fetches* the
  official release media at build time from the FreeBSD archive
  (`http://ftp-archive.freebsd.org/pub/FreeBSD-Archive/old-releases/i386/`) and
  verifies it against the upstream `CHECKSUM.MD5` files shipped alongside each
  distribution set. Fetched media lands in `media/` and emitted images land in
  `dist/`, both of which are **git-ignored** (see `.gitignore`). Nothing large or
  redistributed is committed.
- **Why this version:** FreeBSD 2.2.x (1998) is the closest freely-redistributable
  BSD to the **4.3BSD lineage** of the era's commercial UNIXes. It is a sibling of
  SunOS 4.1.x on the Berkeley CSRG family tree. See `docs/historicity.md`.

## 2. Proprietary OS images — NOT shipped, ever

No SunOS, Solaris, or any other proprietary OS image is included or fetched. The
SunOS *theme* in this box is cosmetic FreeBSD set-dressing (disclosed in
`README.md` and `docs/historicity.md`). If a later box in the series uses a
genuine SunOS image, **the user must obtain that image themselves** — this
project will never redistribute it.

## 3. The historical evidence (referenced, not vendored)

The technique citations in `docs/historicity.md` reference
`~/takedown/TIMELINE.md`, a **sibling repository** holding a forensic
reconstruction of the Feb 1995 Mitnick session playbacks. That repo is treated
as **read-only history**: this project does not merge, submodule, copy, or modify
it. We cite session IDs (`4001`–`4028`) the way one cites a paper. The takedown
repo documents its own provenance and analysis-layer caveats; we inherit those
caveats by reference and do not restate evidence as fact beyond what it supports.

## 5. "Ship nothing real" — scrubbed by design

- **No real credentials or keys.** All accounts use throwaway lab passwords
  documented in the build config. No private keys, no PGP keyrings, no captured
  hashes from the historical record are reproduced.
- **No real proprietary source.** The planted "stolen source" loot tarball
  contains **synthetic placeholder source** themed to evoke the Motorola MANIAC
  firewall material — it is invented for the lab and contains the flag. It is not
  Motorola code, not derived from any real firmware, and is generated by the
  build (never committed; see `.gitignore`).
- **The trojans are reproductions, not the originals.** The setuid `newgrp`
  escalation helper and the period "backdoor" artifacts (the port-5553 /
  port-3111 trojans of the lore) are minimal, from-scratch reproductions written
  for teaching. They are **not** disassembled or recovered originals.

## 6. The backdoors are INERT ARTIFACTS, not live services

The lore's network backdoors (trojan `telnetd` on port 5553, port-3111 shells)
are reproduced **only as discoverable on-disk artifacts to find and analyze** —
disabled binaries, config remnants, log entries. They are **not** wired into
`inetd`/`rc` and do **not** listen. The win condition deliberately requires **no
listening service on the box**: the player exfiltrates the loot *outbound* to
their own host, and possession-after-transfer is the proof. See
`docs/historicity.md` § Safety.

## 7. Reproducibility & integrity

`build/10-fetch-media.sh` records the SHA-256 of every fetched artifact into
`media/MANIFEST.sha256` and checks distribution sets against upstream
`CHECKSUM.MD5`. A clean `make build` from the same upstream archive yields a
functionally identical box. Host-tool prerequisites and the exact QEMU invocation
are pinned in `build/lib.sh` and `config/box.env`.
